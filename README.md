# Signal flags

A small web page for reading and spelling International Code of Signals flag hoists. It runs entirely in your browser.

## What it does

- **Type**: spell a message in flags and read each flag's single-flag meaning. An optional realistic hoist shows how substitutes are used.
- **Flags**: tap flags, including substitutes and the answering pennant, to build a hoist. Read it flag by flag or as a Pub. 102 code group.
- **Dictionary**: search or browse the general and medical signal codes, the U.S./Russia naval appendix, the icebreaker signals, the complement and medical tables, and Chapter 4 (distress, lifesaving and radiotelephone). Filter chips narrow a search by source. Sections start collapsed. Tap a footnote mark to read its note, and tap a cross-reference to jump to it.

Switch between International Code of Signals and US Navy mode, and between three looks: chart room, bridge, and plain. A hoist or any open Dictionary sections will print cleanly from your browser's print command.

## Privacy

Nothing you type is saved or sent anywhere. The page makes no outside requests, sets no cookies and uses no browser storage. Your message, mode and look live only in the page address, so copying the link shares them.

## Source

Signal text is from Pub. 102, International Code of Signals (NGA, 1969 edition revised 2020), a U.S. government work with no copyright claimed. Spellings and punctuation are as printed in that edition, including its typos.

Included, as text: 1,875 signals (1,374 general, with 648 main signals and 726 complements; 445 medical; 56 appendix signals), the single-letter signals, the flag procedure signals, the icebreaker signals, the complement tables, Medical Tables M-1 to M-3, and Chapter 4.

Not included: Morse tables, the Chapter 1 phonetic tables, the Chapter 1 procedure text on sections 6 to 9, the lifesaving pictograms, semaphore, flashing light and sound procedures.

A few codes are absent from the book itself (28 general and 122 medical), so they are absent here too.

## Disclaimers

- This is a reference and learning aid. Do not rely on it for navigation, safety or emergencies.
- It is not affiliated with or endorsed by the National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency or the U.S. Navy.
- Navy single-flag meanings come from publicly circulated sources and are unofficial. Pub. 102 has no Navy single-flag table. Only the U.S./Russia appendix signals are official Navy content in it. Many letters have no public Navy meaning and are left blank on purpose.
- Flag artwork is drawn in code from the standard designs. Navy numerals 6 and 0 and the 4th substitute were checked against fewer sources than the rest.
